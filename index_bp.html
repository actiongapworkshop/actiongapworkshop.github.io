Bridging the Action Gap

Pretrained generative models for robot policy learning — connecting what video, image, and 3D models know about the world to the actions a robot must take in it.

01

Overview

Large-scale generative models pretrained on video, image, and 3D data learn rich representations of objects, spatial structure, physical interactions, and how the visual world changes over time. These models are a promising source of prior knowledge for robot learning, especially because large-scale robot data remains expensive to collect and limited in diversity.

However, most pretraining data contains no robot action labels, while a policy must ultimately produce executable actions that respect the embodiment, dynamics, and sensing constraints of a physical system. This mismatch creates what we call the action gap between generative pretraining and robot policy learning.

A video model can predict how a scene should change, and a 3D generative model can capture the geometry of an environment — but neither directly specifies which robot actions will reliably produce the desired change. This workshop places the interface between generative models and robot policies at the center, asking how models trained to represent or generate changes in the world can help robots learn the actions that cause those changes.

Topics of interest

↳ representation

Latent action learning

Inverse dynamics and action-conditioned generation that link visual change to control.

↳ structure

Object & interaction-centric models

Representations centered on objects, contact, and interaction rather than raw pixels.

↳ prediction

World & action models

Predictive models of dynamics turned into signals for planning and control.

↳ transfer

Cross-embodiment transfer

Grounding disembodied knowledge across robots with different kinematics and sensing.

↳ data

Heterogeneous data sources

Policy learning from human video, simulation, and robot demonstrations together.

↳ evaluation

Evaluating the transfer

Feasibility, controllability, robot-data efficiency, robustness, and inference cost.

02

Core Challenges

Central question: How can knowledge learned from large-scale video, image, and 3D data collected without robot action labels be converted into executable robot policies? The workshop addresses this through five concrete open challenges.

1

The right intermediate abstraction

What action-relevant representations can be learned from action-free data? Should visual change connect to control through latent actions, object motion, contact states, task-space trajectories, or something else — and how do these trade off?

2

Grounding in embodiment

How can representations be grounded in the kinematic, dynamic, and sensing constraints of different embodiments, so what is learned from disembodied data stays physically realizable?

3

How much robot supervision is needed?

How much paired robot data is necessary to bridge the gap? Which forms of supervision give the greatest value per unit of collection cost? Is action-free or action-light policy learning the realistic objective?

4

From open-loop generation to closed-loop control

How can models trained for open-loop visual or 3D generation become policies capable of closed-loop execution, correction, and recovery when predictions diverge from real observations?

5

How should transfer be evaluated?

What criteria fit — action feasibility, controllability, data efficiency, task success, generalization, robustness to distribution shift, inference cost — and how should the trade-offs among them be assessed?

Call for Papers

Submit your work

We solicit extended abstracts and short papers of up to four pages through OpenReview. Submissions are non-archival. We welcome new, preliminary, and in-progress work — including negative results, empirical comparisons, and evaluation methodologies.

  • Each submission receives at least two reviews from organizers and an invited program committee.
  • Submissions are assessed on relevance to the workshop challenges and potential to stimulate discussion.
  • All accepted papers are presented as posters and integrated into the breakout sessions.
  • A Best Paper Award and possible Honorable Mentions recognize strong contributions.
  • Per the Call for Workshops, papers already accepted to the CoRL 2026 main conference are not eligible.
