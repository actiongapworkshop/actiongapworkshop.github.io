About

Large-scale generative models pretrained on video, image, and 3D data learn rich representations of objects, spatial structure, physical interactions, and how the visual world changes over time. These models are a promising source of prior knowledge for robot learning, especially because large-scale robot data remains expensive to collect and limited in diversity.

However, most pretraining data contains no robot action labels, while a policy must ultimately produce executable actions that respect the embodiment, dynamics, and sensing constraints of a physical system. This mismatch creates an action gap between generative pretraining and robot policy learning.

A video model can predict how a scene should change, and a 3D generative model can capture the geometry of an environment — but neither directly specifies which robot actions will reliably produce the desired change. G2A places the interface between generative models and robot policies at the center, asking how models trained to represent or generate changes in the world can help robots learn the actions that cause those changes.

Central question

How can knowledge learned from large-scale video, image, and 3D data collected without robot action labels be converted into executable robot policies?

Call for Papers

We invite submissions on any aspect of turning pretrained generative models into robot policies. We welcome new, preliminary, and in-progress work — including negative results, empirical comparisons, position papers, benchmarks, and evaluation methodologies.

Topics of interest

  • Latent action learning. Inverse dynamics, action-conditioned generation, and latent action spaces learned from action-free video that link visual change to control.
  • Object- and interaction-centric models. Representations centered on objects, contact, affordances, and interaction rather than raw pixels, and how they expose actionable structure.
  • World and action models. Predictive models of dynamics turned into signals for planning, model-predictive control, and policy learning.
  • Cross-embodiment transfer. Grounding disembodied knowledge in the kinematic, dynamic, and sensing constraints of robots so that what is learned stays physically realizable.
  • Heterogeneous data sources. Policy learning that combines human video, simulation, and robot demonstrations, including alignment and curation across sources.
  • From open-loop generation to closed-loop control. Turning generative rollouts into policies capable of execution, correction, and recovery when predictions diverge from real observations.
  • Robot data efficiency. Action-free and action-light policy learning, and which forms of supervision give the greatest value per unit of collection cost.
  • Evaluation. Benchmarks and protocols measuring action feasibility, controllability, robot-data efficiency, generalization, robustness to distribution shift, and inference cost.

Submission tracks

Track 1

Research papers

Novel methods, empirical studies, algorithmic advances, benchmarks, datasets, or system demonstrations that bridge generative pretraining and robot action.

4–9 pages + references
Track 2

Position & negative-result papers

Opinionated positions on the right abstractions or evaluation criteria, reproducibility studies, and rigorous negative results that the community should know about.

4–9 pages + references

Submission guidelines

Submissions should follow the CoRL 2026 LaTeX template and be between 4 and 9 pages, excluding references and appendices. All papers must be submitted as anonymized PDFs for double-blind review via OpenReview, with author names, affiliations, and acknowledgments removed and prior work cited in the third person.

Accepted papers will be featured in a poster session, with a small number selected for oral presentation. Proceedings are non-archival, allowing future conference or journal submissions; work under review at or accepted by other venues is welcome, though CoRL 2026 main-conference papers are not eligible.
